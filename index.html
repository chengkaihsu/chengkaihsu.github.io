About

About Me

I am an Incoming Assistant Professor in the School of Geographical Sciences and Urban Planning at Arizona State University.

My research lies at the intersection of transportation, climate change, environmental health, and urban planning. I study how transportation systems shape exposure to environmental hazards such as extreme heat and air pollution, and how these exposures influence road safety, health, and equity. My work combines methods from epidemiology, transportation research, urban analytics, and causal inference to generate evidence that can inform healthier and more sustainable cities.

A major focus of my research examines how heat affects road traffic injuries and fatalities across different transportation modes, population groups, and urban contexts. I am also interested in understanding how transportation and climate policies—including fleet electrification, active transportation, and urban greening—can improve population health and reduce environmental inequalities.

I completed my PhD in City and Regional Planning at the University of California, Berkeley, where I also served as a postdoctoral researcher at the Institute of Transportation Studies. Prior to Berkeley, I earned an MSc in Transport from Imperial College London and a BS in Urban Planning from National Cheng Kung University in Taiwan.

Research

Research Agenda

01

Heat, Mobility, and Road Safety

I examine how ambient temperature and extreme heat affect road traffic injuries and fatalities across transportation modes, demographic groups, and urban contexts.

02

Air Pollution and Transportation

My work evaluates how transportation policies, fleet electrification, and urban mobility interventions may reduce PM2.5 exposure and improve public health.

03

Urban Climate and Health Equity

I study urban heat island intensity and how city form, land use, and transportation systems contribute to environmental disparities.

04

Health Impact Quantification

I develop transparent and transferable frameworks for estimating the health benefits of climate and transportation interventions in cities.
