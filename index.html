About

About me

I am an Assistant Professor in the School of Geographical Sciences and Urban Planning at Arizona State University. My research sits at the intersection of transportation, climate change, environmental health, and urban planning.

A central thread of my work asks how heat affects road traffic injuries and deaths, and why those effects differ by travel mode, age, sex, income, and urban context. Using data from hundreds of cities in Latin America, Taiwan, and the United States, I combine methods from injury epidemiology, transportation research, urban analytics, and causal inference to produce evidence that can guide climate adaptation and Vision Zero strategies.

I am also interested in how transportation and climate policies—fleet electrification, active travel, urban greening, and safer street design—can reduce exposure to air pollution and heat, improve population health, and narrow environmental inequities. Earlier work examined how algorithmic incentives and heat jointly shape risky riding among on-demand food-delivery motorcyclists.

Before joining ASU, I was a postdoctoral researcher at the Institute of Transportation Studies, UC Berkeley, where I also completed my PhD in City and Regional Planning. I hold an MSc in Transport from Imperial College London and University College London and a BS in Urban Planning from National Cheng Kung University, Taiwan.

Research

Research themes

Four connected lines of work, each linking a transportation or climate exposure to a measurable health outcome.

01

Heat, mobility & road safety

How ambient and nighttime heat raise the risk of crashes, injuries, and deaths—and who is most affected: motorcyclists, cyclists, pedestrians, older adults, and residents of lower-income and high-conflict neighborhoods.

Latin America · Taiwan · California

02

Air pollution & transportation

Exposure to PM2.5 among commuters and micro-mobility users, neighborhood disparities in pollution across Latin American cities, and the health gains available from cleaner fleets and mode shift.

340 Latin American cities · London · Kaohsiung

03

Urban climate & health equity

Urban heat island intensity and how city form, land use, and transportation infrastructure concentrate environmental burdens in particular places and populations.

SALURBAL collaboration

04

Policy evaluation & health impact quantification

Quasi-experimental evaluations of road-safety policies (e.g., Taiwan's non-yielding penalty amendment) and transparent, transferable frameworks for estimating the health benefits of climate and transportation interventions.

Transport Policy · Cities
